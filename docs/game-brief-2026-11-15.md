# The game: creative brief, 15 November 2026

The shareable copy is the private page "Feetness Creative Brief" on claude.ai; this file is the repo's record. The model is decided; names and art are open. Nothing here is built yet; the foundation is phase 2 in `PLAN.md` section 5.

## What Feetness is

You text what you trained. The app saves every set as a fact, keeps a written profile that cites your own messages, tracks injuries, and coaches you from what you logged. The game layer is the way those facts are shown and talked about. It is not a second engine: every level, bar and "ready" comes from a number the code computed. A chatbot gives advice and forgets you. Feetness remembers, keeps score, and plays it.

## The three energies

| Energy (working name) | In plain words | Master | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat | the one that lifts: force, the fight, the next heavier set | Lt. Surge | clipped, loads first, never cruel |
| Current | the one that lasts: the nerve and the spark that keeps going | Shen | thinks in phases and campaigns |
| Root | the one that heals: what grows back, sleep, rest, mobility | Latika | warm, reads how you feel first |

The energies affect each other, and the effects are training science: hard Heat without Root burns out (no sleep or rest days: gains stall, injury risk rises); Heat and Current compete for one week (pushing both hard blunts strength gains, the interference effect); Root is when the other two grow (gains land after rest). The type chart with these three is the one screen that explains the game. The reference was fire, lightning and grass-water energies from creature games; that is inspiration, never the product. The energies get their own names and symbols.

## The crew: six members, two per energy

| Working name | Energy | Quality | What trains it | The measure behind the level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anvil | Heat | strength | compound lifts, the hinge, carries | estimated one-rep max relative to body weight (Epley, sets of up to 10 reps) |
| Flint | Heat | power | sprints, jumps, fast sets | sprint and jump tests; the weakest measure, later |
| Drift | Current | endurance | easy minutes, long runs, bike, rows | weekly easy minutes against 150; longest run of 30 days; a 12-minute test every four weeks |
| Keel | Current | core and drive | planks, carries, hanging raises | longest hold, carry distance and load |
| Willow | Root | mobility | squat depth, hinges, overhead reach, toe touch | yes/no field tests the person logs |
| Ember | Root | recovery | sleep, rest days, deloads | sleep hours when told, rest days, how an injury is going |

Direction "the crew": the body as a working crew, each member a trade. Considered and set aside: an expedition (bends toward races), elemental spirits (too close to what we must not copy). All names open.

## Three numbers per member

| Number | What it is | Moves | Real thing behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level | how much that member has trained, ever | only up; rest and injury take nothing | training history: sets, minutes, sessions over time |
| Power | what that member can do today | up with training, down with weeks off or an injury | the measure above, over a recent window |
| HP | how ready that member is to work today | drops the day after it worked, back within a day or two; low after short sleep or illness | muscle recovery after a session (about 48 to 72 hours for a hard one), sleep, rest |

Training science splits them the same way: fitness builds and fades slowly, what you can produce today moves with fatigue and detraining. A level 38 Anvil at 60 percent power since a back strain is an honest story with a promise in it; the comeback is computed, not written. HP is per member: deadlifts yesterday, Anvil low today and fine tomorrow, Drift fresh. Ember feeds every member's HP, so Latika owns it. Readiness for a battle reads Power; promotions and the weekly chronicle read Level.

## The loop

A day: the morning message names who takes the field ("Anvil and Keel today: bench and planks. Drift rests, she ran yesterday"); the person trains and texts one line; the receipt says who worked; HP drops for them tonight. A week: the roster shows who was busy and who idle ("idle", never "losing"); later one talent point a week says which energy leads. A battle: a goal with a date; the screen shows what it demands and how ready each member is; inside two weeks taper and rest, further out build; on the day a test or the event, the result moves power, a milestone earns a promotion. Sunday: the chronicle, the week as an episode in the coach's voice with the real numbers.

## Battles: three worked examples

| Battle | Demands most | Two weeks out | Two months out |
|---|---|---|---|
| A rave on 13 December | Drift, Ember, Willow | endurance cannot be built in time; arrive rested: taper, sleep, light movement | easy minutes build Drift; the last ten days taper |
| A 5k in February | Drift first, Keel second | the longest-run-plus-10-percent rule, run-walk | a base, then one hard interval session a week after four weeks |
| Carrying a fridge next month | Anvil, Keel, Willow | hinge and carries at moderate load; no new maxes the week before | a hinge progression |

Underneath: specificity, the taper (about half the volume for one to two weeks, intensity kept), fitness and fatigue, the 10 percent rule for running, sleep. Each rule gets a source line in a method card before it ships.

## Non-negotiables

- Every number is computed by code from what the person logged; the story never invents one.
- Rest and injury never cost progress; no streak that shames.
- Warning signs stop the game and say to see a doctor.
- Logging stays one line of text.
- No diagnosis, no medication doses, no nutrition in this phase.
- Nothing borrowed from existing creature games: names, creatures, element types, badges, looks.
- A person's name and identity never go to the AI.
